# Where every line of wording on the site comes from

This note checks every line of text on the site against the two client documents:

- **Commentary** = "Artworks – Mr. Balu" (the Harmonious Science commentary)
- **Essay** = "Two Approaches of Art – HS"
- **Article** = "Renowned Artist K. Balasubramanian", yathraemagazine.com, 8 December 2023. It contains the artist's own first-person statements and his biography.

Each line is given one of four labels:

- **Exact quote**: word for word from the PDF or article. "…" marks only where a sentence is cut.
- **Close to the PDF**: the PDF's own words, lightly shortened or with a few words joined together. The meaning is unchanged.
- **Used for one painting**: taken from the PDF, but the PDF says it about his work in general or about several paintings at once. It is shown here under one particular painting.
- **Written for the site**: not in the PDFs. These are section labels, headings and button text.

## One thing to know first

Neither PDF mentions Mr. Balu by name in the text itself. His name appears only in the file name "Artworks-Mr.Balu". The site shows his full name, **K. Balasubramanian**, which comes from the yathraemagazine.com article, not from the PDFs. The essay also never says which artist follows "The Path Through Simplicity". That link comes from the client brief. The essay does describe circles, triangles and open space, which matches the commentary on his paintings.

## Corrections made during this check

Three lines on the site sounded more certain than the PDF, or joined sentences in a way that changed the meaning. They now match the PDF:

1. **Nine Colours.** The PDF says "*Almost like* unity expressing itself through *qualitative* diversity". The site had left out "almost like" and "qualitative". Both are back.
2. **Colour element.** The same fix, because this line is taken from the same passage.
3. **Pancha-bhutha.** The site had joined two sentences into one claim about the painting. They are now two statements, as in the PDF: the painting shows levels, and the Aagamas treat the Pancha-bhuthas as states of manifestation.

## Worth checking with the client

- **Two readings of the triangle.** The artist says his triangles express Shakti, the union of Purusha and Prakriti, and the union of the Atman with the Paramatman. The HS commentary says one "may think" of Shiva or Shakti, but reads the triangle "differently", as "the minimum geometry capable of sustaining transformation". The site shows both, each clearly attributed: his words in the Paintings section, the HS reading in The Work and The Elements. The client may want to confirm they're comfortable with both appearing.
- **Painting stories.** Ask the artist for a line on each painting in his own words, and on why he made it. That is the only way to give each painting a true story of its own.

- **Which photograph is which painting.** Ask the client to name the Nine Colours, Yin-Yang, Pancha-bhutha, green, blue and red paintings among the photographs, so their descriptions can go back on.
- **Blue and red paintings.** Their kept descriptions use passages the PDFs apply to his work in general. If the client wants only lines the PDF says about each painting, all three (green, blue, red) should share the single commentary passage about open space.
- **Headings written for the site.** "Each work, on its own" and "Reduced to relationships" are not in the PDFs. They can be changed or removed.
